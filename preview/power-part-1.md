I’ve heard over and over that power accumulates and corrupts. And I believe this is true! But I don’t have a similar gut-level understanding of why this happens. I think of power as a conniving otherworldly actor that is always one step ahead. So, in part 1 of this essay I am going to try and explain why power works this way. In part 2 I'll lay out a few principles for using power well. Modern institutions are essentially 'power infrastructure,' so it should be worthwhile to build out our mental model of how power works, and why.

First, a terminology note: the word 'power' has baggage; also there isn't a good term for one who has or uses power — power holder? power broker? power using person? All too clunky. So I'll use these words:
- A force is something that exerts power without intent
- An agent is something that intentionally exerts power to some purpose
- Agency is power wielded by an agent

These terms also demystify power and get to the heart of what it is: the capacity to shape reality.[^1]
## What shapes reality
People can be powerful. Ideas, such as communism or capitalism are seen as influential or powerful. Technology, like weapons or the internet, is powerful. Natural phenomena, like storms, are powerful. All shape reality. 

Natural phenomena, like a tornado, are great examples of direct forces. A tornado will rip up a tree and toss it a mile away. But tornados do not have intent like people do. They don’t intentionally clear a field so the next wind-storm can carry a bunch of corn seeds into the freshly tilled ground so it has a neatly planted mono-cropped field it can use to feed livestock.

Technology, up until recently, has been a latent force.[^2] And while it doesn’t have intent, it implies intent. It has a purpose, but it has to be catalyzed by an agent. An espresso machine has the potential to transform ground coffee beans and hot water into espresso, but it needs a person to realize its potential. The same goes for more obviously powerful technologies, like bombs. Technology is contingent in a way tornadoes are not.

Ideas are indirect forces. Instead of directly clearing a forest like a tornado, ideas will do it by convincing people corn syrup is useful. Those people will want to buy corn, therefore causing someone else to clear a field and plant a neat, mono-cropped field. Ideas have lower activation energy than most technologies. Our espresso machine requires active effort: buying coffee beans, grinding them to the correct size, hooking the machine up to water and electricity, paying the utility bills. Ideas don't need any of that. They are more like viruses — other than being able to receive them, the host doesn't have to do much for ideas to shape reality through them. A host will pick up an idea from a podcast or essay, and willingly proselytize it to others. But ideas don't have intent. Like the tornado, they don't actually care about the mono-cropped field. Technologies and ideas both need people. Technologies must be used to realize their power, while ideas realize their power by using people.[^3]

Even though they lack intent, some of the most powerful forces are ideas, like capitalism, communism, or Christianity. This is because they can quickly influence a lot of agents. Capitalism doesn’t directly write laws to enable free trade. But politicians who believe capitalism is useful, do. Agents also use this trick — a boss directs employees toward a goal, and provides technology so the employee can quickly accomplish the goal. This is how power works — an agent doesn't usually have a million times more power than another agent; instead a system allows one agent to direct a million other agents and technologies toward its goals.
## How agents interact
Absolute agency is not zero sum. The total agency, or power wielded by agents, has grown a lot. In the past, if a person wanted a building, they had to build it by hand. Today a person can rent a bulldozer and use power tools. In the future, maybe you'll be able to rent a robot that will autonomously build a building. A person's absolute ability to shape reality has gone up, and that doesn't inherently reduce other agents’ agency.

Relative agency, however, is zero sum. If agents ever have competing priorities, it is not enough to simply have more absolute agency than people hundreds of years ago. You must have more agency than your rival. Just because a person can rent a bulldozer and use power tools doesn’t mean they can ignore the permitting process or zoning laws without consequence. Even though their absolute power is incredible, their relative power might be lower than it was a few hundred years ago (e.g., there are zoning laws dictating what you can and cannot do on your property).[^4]

Agents can use their agency to get more agency. They'll do this because they have goals, and will inevitably run into barriers along the way, and additional power is useful for overcoming these barriers. Sadly, agents will inevitably compete. The world contains so many agents that their interests will eventually diverge, and other agents, other people, will become barriers to another agent's goal.

Say our bulldozer renter is expanding her septic field because she wants to build an in-law suite. However, she realizes the zoning laws do not permit her to do so. Simply getting a bigger bulldozer does not solve her problem. She needs to change the system to accomplish her goal. She attempts to do this from her position as a citizen. She probably writes to the zoning board and explains why she should be able to build an in-law suite. But her petition is ignored, so now she has to increase her relative power, or give up her goal. So, she decides to invest the in-law suite funds to run for local office to empower property holders by removing zoning legislation. 

Marginal agency over a rival agent is always useful. So, agents will deploy power to increase their power relative to other agents. I'm sure you can imagine how this might play out when an agent directs the effort of a million other agents toward increasing their power relative to their rivals.
## Why does power accumulate and corrupt?
Let's start with accumulation. Power accumulates because it is useful — it helps agents achieve their goals. But, is power accumulating actually bad? Is accumulated power inherently corrupt? I think the answer is, no. Power, in and of itself, is not bad. It is something that can be used for either good or bad, and often, the good outweighs the bad. Because power accumulates we can build things faster, cure diseases, address food insecurity, and extend lives. These are all very good things.[^5]

And my religion, Christianity, affirms this position. God is powerful and good. Interestingly, the God of the Bible, to the extent he is scrutable, seems to optimize for increasing agency. He creates the universe and delegates his authority over it to humans — he commands Adam and Eve to work the ground.[^6] And God cares about increasing agency so much, that he tolerates us using it for bad.[^7] We get a glimpse of his soul after the flood narrative. God resolves never to wipe out humanity again, even though they are wicked. And so he sets out to restore humans into wise agents who will use power for good, and does so primarily through… humans. So much so that God eventually becomes a human.

So, if accumulated power isn't inherently corrupt, and might even be the good that God optimizes for, why does power corrupt? Power corrupts at the seam where absolute agency becomes relative agency. And the seam is rivalry.[^8] Think about it: relative power should be an irrelevant fact about the world. Objects come in different sizes. There are dandelions, and there are oaks. And if you optimize for absolute life, both are treasured. But, if you optimize for being the biggest plant, all of a sudden relative size is very important. Rivalry makes relative power salient. When two parties have the shared goal of increasing absolute power, they’ll respect and celebrate the power of both parties. They'll willingly cede relative power to increase absolute power.  But once they see the other as a rival, they'll begin to compete. They view the other’s power with suspicion, and begin to optimize for relative power. And this is particularly easy to slide into if you have a lot of power. First, you have power at your disposal, and second, you have more to lose. Once an agent adopts rivalry, these facts pull it toward corruption.

This is why power corrupts. Rivalry makes you look on others with suspicion. And corruption is most manifest when the suspicion leads you to maximize your relative power, even at the expense of absolute power.[^9] In fact, rivalry, concern for relative power, is the original sin. In Eden, the snake gets Adam and Eve to wonder if God is a rival. After all, why would he tell them not to eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil? <br> 
	"Because he knows that when you eat from it your eyes will be opened, and you will become like God, knowing good and evil."

It seems like God might be holding out on them — and this lens, this fear is what leads them to take the fruit. And the dynamic plays out in the next generation. God favors Abel's offering. In a world where Cain cared about absolute agency, this would have been a reason to celebrate. Instead, Cain sees Abel as a rival, and murders him. This is power corrupted — wielded to decrease absolute power in the name of relative power.

A world without rivalry is one where relative power is irrelevant. Absolute power is pursued because it is useful in the giving of gifts, not because it gives us an advantage over other agents. And ideally power flows to where it is needed, without concern for standing. Relative agency goes back to being an irrelevant fact.

But our world today is one where relative power is highly salient. And yet, it is a world where absolute agency is increasing at an ever quickening rate. Even more puzzling, it is rivalry —capitalist competition — that is the principal engine driving the growth in absolute agency. Somehow, rivalry, the snake's animating force, is being used for good.<br>
	"As for you, you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good, to bring it about that many people should be kept alive, as they are today."
